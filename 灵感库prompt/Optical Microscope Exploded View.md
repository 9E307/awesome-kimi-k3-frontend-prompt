# 标题
Optical Microscope Exploded View

## 演示链接
https://k3-microscope.ok.kimi.link/

## 提示词
Create an interactive 3D compound microscope that users can both take apart and operate. Present it as a detailed laboratory instrument in a dark studio, with an optional light studio theme. Include a binocular head, two eyepieces, a four-position rotating objective turret, a mechanical stage with a glass slide, an Abbe condenser, an iris diaphragm, coaxial coarse and fine focus knobs, an illuminator, an arm, and a weighted base.

Give the microscope realistic materials: lightly textured powder-coated metal, chrome objective barrels, black stage coating, rubber eyecups and focus grips, brass gears, and optical glass with restrained green and violet reflections. Add magnification bands, printed objective markings, knurled rings, slide clips, stage scales, screws, seams, and a power panel. Reveal the internal lens groups, binocular prisms, rack-and-pinion focusing mechanism, eight-blade iris, LED reflector, and turret detent ball and spring during disassembly.

Provide an exploded-view slider, assemble and explode controls, automatic demonstration, spin pause, and component labels with subtle hover highlighting. Stage the teardown carefully: move the objectives aside first, lift the slide clear, then withdraw the stage before releasing the internal lens elements. All parts should move smoothly without passing through one another, and each objective must align with the optical axis when rotated into the working position.

In the assembled working mode, let users drag the turret to switch between 4×, 10×, 40×, and 100× objectives, with damped movement, 90-degree detents, and quiet mechanical clicks. Drag the focus knobs to adjust focus, with a fine-adjustment modifier; drag the stage to scan the specimen; adjust the condenser iris; and rotate the eyepiece diopter ring. Include a circular eyepiece view that can be opened by clicking the eyepiece or using a dedicated control.

Offer onion epidermis, cheek cells, spirogyra, and yeast specimens. Update both the slide and eyepiece image when the specimen changes. The eyepiece view should respond to magnification, focus, stage position, and aperture: higher magnification narrows the field, moving the slide shifts the image in the opposite direction, defocus softens detail and reduces contrast, and closing the iris darkens the image while slightly increasing contrast. Include an adaptive scale bar, subtle edge softness and vignetting, and a clear focus indicator. Support keyboard controls for focus, iris adjustment, specimen selection, and the eyepiece view.
