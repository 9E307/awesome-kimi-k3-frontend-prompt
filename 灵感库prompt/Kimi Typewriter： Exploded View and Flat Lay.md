# 标题
Kimi Typewriter: Exploded View and Flat Lay

## 演示链接
https://k3-typewriter.ok.kimi.link/

## 提示词
Create an interactive 3D mechanical study of a Smith-Corona-style portable typewriter with Kimi branding. Present it in a warm, light photography studio with an optional dark theme. Use a prewar-inspired finish: satin black enamel, subtle gold-toned nickel trim, ivory keycaps, polished steel mechanisms, a rubber platen, and a black-and-red ribbon. Give the shell gently formed surfaces, rolled edges, restrained reflections, and visible machining marks on the metal parts.

Build a detailed 572-part interpretive reconstruction, organized into eight functional groups: keys, levers, typebars, ribbon, carriage, frame, hardware, and shell. Include 50 keys, 44 fan-arranged typebars, the slotted typebar basket, carriage, paper bail, scale, carriage-return lever, and small screws, washers, pins, brackets, and linkages. Use the disassembly reference photograph to guide individual outlines and proportions. Identify uncertain reconstructions clearly and describe the result as a photographic mechanical study rather than factory repair CAD or a verified parts inventory.

Let a single progress control move continuously through three states: fully assembled machine, exploded mechanism, and a dense, orderly overhead flat lay. Separate the shell and carriage first, then reveal the ribbon, typebars, keys, levers, frame, and hardware. Arrange all 572 pieces in a balanced 16:9 composition, keeping related parts grouped and leaving visible gaps between neighboring pieces. Use carefully staged rotations and smooth movement, with no overlapping pieces in the finished flat lay.

Provide automatic demonstration, play and pause, reset to the complete machine, a direct flat-lay control, and a typing-strike demonstration. Pressing a letter should animate the corresponding key, linkage, and typebar together. Include a search field and filters for all eight groups. Hovering should show the part name and identifier; selecting a part should open a close inspection with its description and group, plus previous and next navigation within that group. Returning from inspection should restore the earlier view.

Let users scrub disassembly with the progress slider, scroll, or drag; rotate the view with a modifier or right-button drag; and zoom with a modifier or touch pinch. Support keyboard shortcuts for typing, theme switching, playback, and reset. Move the camera smoothly from a three-quarter view of the assembled machine to an overhead view of the flat lay, keeping every part within the visible area and clear of the controls.
